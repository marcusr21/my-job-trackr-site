Ghosted After Applying? How Long to Wait and What to Send

Published 27 September 2026 · 6 min read

By Marcus · Founder, My Job Trackr

Published: September 27, 2026

Being ghosted after applying for a job is one of the most frustrating parts of any job search. You put hours into an application, maybe even a couple of interviews, and then nothing. No update, no rejection, just silence.

If it has happened to you, you are far from alone. This guide covers how long to wait before following up, exactly what to send (with copy-and-paste templates), and how to stop one silent employer from stalling your whole search.

How Common Is Employer Ghosting?

Very. In Greenhouse's 2024 survey of 2,500 workers across the US, UK and Germany, 61% of job seekers said they had been ghosted after a job interview (Greenhouse). A 2026 survey by Criteria found 53% of job seekers had been ghosted within the previous year, up from 38% in 2024 (Fortune).

In a busy market, recruiters are handling a lot of applicants for every role, and silence is often down to volume, internal delays or a role being paused, not anything you did. That does not make it fair. But it does mean it is rarely personal.

How Long Should You Wait Before Following Up?

There are no official rules, but these timings are sensible and polite:

  • After submitting an application: wait until at least a week after the closing date. If there is no closing date, wait around two weeks after applying.
  • After a first interview: if they gave you a timeline, wait until it has passed, then allow two more working days. If they did not, follow up after about a week.
  • After a final interview: follow up after five working days, or the day after the date they said they would decide.
  • After a follow-up with no reply: send one more, a week later. After that, move on.

Log each date as you go. A job tracker with follow-up reminders means you never have to remember who you chased and when.

What to Send: 3 Follow-Up Templates

Keep follow-ups short, specific and friendly. Send them to the person you have been dealing with, whether that is the recruiter or the hiring manager.

Template 1: No response after applying

Subject: Application for [Job Title], [Your Name]

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Job Title] role on [date] and wanted to check whether there is an update on the timeline. I am still very interested in the role, particularly [one specific thing about the team or company].

Happy to send anything else that would help.

Thanks,
[Your Name]

Template 2: No response after an interview

Subject: [Job Title] interview on [date]

Hi [Name],

Thank you again for the interview on [date]. I enjoyed hearing about [specific topic from the interview]. I wanted to check in on the next steps and whether there is anything else you need from me.

Best wishes,
[Your Name]

Template 3: Final follow-up (closing the loop)

Subject: Re: [Job Title]

Hi [Name],

I have not heard back about the [Job Title] role, so I will assume you have gone in a different direction. Thank you for your time. I would be glad to be considered for future roles at [Company].

All the best,
[Your Name]

The final follow-up is surprisingly effective. It is polite and it closes the loop, and it sometimes prompts a reply when nothing else did.

What to Do When You Have Been Ghosted

Step 1: Check your inbox and spam folder

Automated rejections and interview invites regularly land in spam or promotions tabs. Search for the company name before assuming the worst.

Step 2: Send one follow-up at the right time

Use the timings and templates above. One well-timed message is better than three rushed ones.

Step 3: Send a final follow-up a week later

If there is still no answer, send the closing-the-loop message. Then mark the application as closed in your tracker.

Step 4: Keep applying in parallel

Never pause your search while waiting on one employer. The best protection against ghosting is having several live applications at once.

How to Stop Ghosting Derailing Your Job Search

Ghosting hurts most when one application is carrying all your hope. A few habits make it much easier to shrug off:

  • Keep a steady pipeline. Aim to always have several applications in progress, so no single silence stops your momentum.
  • Track every stage. When you can see every role, its stage and its next follow-up date in one place, a quiet employer becomes a task, not a worry. That is the whole idea behind job tracking.
  • Look for patterns. If you are regularly ghosted after first interviews, that is useful data. It might point to interview prep, salary expectations or the types of roles you are targeting.
  • Protect your energy. Set a time each day for job hunting, and switch off outside it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I wait to hear back after a job interview?▾

After a job interview, wait until the date the employer gave you has passed, then allow two more working days before following up. If no timeline was given, a follow-up after about one week is reasonable. Send one more message a week later if you still hear nothing, then move on.

Is it normal to be ghosted after an interview?▾

Being ghosted after an interview is common. In Greenhouse's 2024 survey of 2,500 workers across the US, UK and Germany, 61% of job seekers said they had been ghosted after a job interview. It usually reflects delays, high applicant volumes or a paused role rather than anything the candidate did.

How many times should I follow up on a job application?▾

Follow up on a job application a maximum of two times. Send the first follow-up about a week after the closing date or after the timeline you were given, and a final, polite follow-up a week later. After that, mark the application as closed and focus on your other roles.

What should I say in a follow-up email after no response?▾

A follow-up email after no response should be short: name the role and the date you applied or interviewed, say you are still interested, mention one specific detail about the role or team, and ask if there is an update on the timeline. Keep it to four or five sentences. Tools like My Job Trackr can remind you when each follow-up is due.

Silence Is Not a Verdict

Being ghosted after applying or interviewing is common, and it usually says more about the employer's process than about you. Wait a sensible amount of time, send a short follow-up, close the loop if you still hear nothing, and keep your other applications moving.
