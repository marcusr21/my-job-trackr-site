How Many Job Applications Does It Take to Get a Job in the UK?

Short answer: there is no official average, but competition is high. UK employers received 140 applications per graduate vacancy (ISE, 2023/24), and there are about 2.5 unemployed people for every vacancy (ONS, September 2026). Track your own numbers to see what it takes for you.
Published 27 September 2026 · 6 min read

By Marcus · Founder, My Job Trackr

Published: September 27, 2026

If you have sent 30, 50 or even 100 applications and still do not have an offer, you have probably wondered how many job applications it takes to get a job. It is one of the most searched questions in any job hunt, and the honest answer is that nobody publishes an official average.

What we do have is solid data on how much competition there is for each role. In this post we will look at the latest UK numbers, what they actually mean, and how to work out your own figure so you can see whether your approach is working.

The Short Answer

There is no official UK statistic for how many applications the average person sends before landing a job. It depends heavily on your sector, seniority, location and how targeted your applications are.

But the competition figures are clear. Graduate employers received an average of 140 applications per vacancy in 2023/24, and there are roughly 2.5 unemployed people for every open vacancy in the UK right now. In other words, a lot of people are applying for each role, so a high number of applications before an offer is normal.

What the Latest UK Data Shows

Applications per vacancy: graduate roles

The Institute of Student Employers found that employers received an average of 140 applications per graduate job in 2023/24, a 59% rise in a single year and the highest since it began collecting data in 1991 (ISE). Competition varied a lot by sector:

Unemployed people per vacancy: the whole market

The ONS reported 702,000 vacancies in June to August 2026, the lowest level outside the pandemic since 2014 (ONS). Around 1.78 million people were unemployed in May to July 2026 (House of Commons Library). Divide one by the other and you get about 2.5 unemployed people for every vacancy.

That ratio understates the competition you face. People who already have a job apply for roles too, and most people apply for many roles at once.

Applications per job on recruitment platforms

Recruitment software provider Tribepad reported an average of 48.7 applications per job on its platform in November 2024, based on more than 4.5 million applications (Tribepad). That figure comes from one platform's customers, but it shows how crowded the average advert has become.

Why Your Number Is Different From Anyone Else's

Two people can send the same number of applications and get completely different results. The biggest factors are:

  • How targeted you are. Fewer, tailored applications to roles you match well usually convert better than mass applying.
  • Your sector. Using the ISE figures, a graduate role in digital and IT attracts almost three times the applications of one in the charity and public sector.
  • Where you apply. Referrals, direct applications and smaller employers often have less competition than roles on the biggest job boards.
  • Your stage. Early-career roles attract more applicants per vacancy than specialist, experienced roles.

How to Work Out Your Own Numbers

The most useful benchmark is your own. Here is how to calculate it.

Step 1: Log every application

Record the company, role, date and where you applied, for every single application. Without a complete record, your numbers will be guesswork.

Step 2: Track each stage

Mark each application as it moves forward: applied, responded, interview, final interview, offer or rejected. A job tracker makes this a one-click update rather than a spreadsheet chore.

Step 3: Calculate your conversion rates

Divide the number that reached each stage by the number at the stage before. For example, interviews ÷ applications gives your interview rate.

Step 4: Find the weakest stage and fix it

A low response rate points to your CV or targeting. A low interview-to-offer rate points to interview preparation. Change one thing at a time and watch the numbers.

An illustration (not a benchmark)

Say you send 60 applications over six weeks. Eight lead to a first interview, three to a final interview and one to an offer. Your interview rate is 13% (8 ÷ 60) and your final-to-offer rate is 33% (1 ÷ 3). If your first-interview rate is the weak point, that is where to focus, not on sending more applications.

How to Get More From Fewer Applications

  • Tailor the top third of your CV to each role's key requirements.
  • Apply early. Many adverts close once enough applications arrive.
  • Follow up once, politely, if you hear nothing (here is what to send if you have been ghosted).
  • Keep a steady weekly target instead of bursts of mass applications.
  • Review your conversion rates every week and adjust.
